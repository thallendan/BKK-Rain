ℹ️ What do these top cards mean? (ranges & extremes)

Regime now

Which way the low-level air (850 hPa, about 1.5 km up, above ground friction) is flowing across the Bangkok/Gulf/Andaman area. That air carries the moisture and steers storms. "Running" = hours the same regime has lasted. Long runs mean a stable pattern; a change often marks a shift in the weather.

  • SW (westerly): moist air off the Andaman Sea/Bay of Bengal. The normal rainy-season flow (May–Oct). Stronger and longer = more widespread, persistent rain.
  • NE: cool, dry air from the China/Siberian high (Nov–Feb). Usually dry and cooler in Bangkok. A strong surge can pick up Gulf moisture and bring rain to the east and south coasts.
  • E / SE: air from the Pacific and South China Sea. Humid. Often seen with typhoons passing to the east or north, and around the season changes. Can give afternoon storms or rain from the east.
  • N: dry continental air from the north. Dry and cool.
  • WEAK: light winds under 2.5 m/s. Rain is then mostly local sea-breeze thunderstorms and harder to predict.

Core wind 850 (m/s)

  • Under 3: weak. 3–7: moderate. 7–12: strong. 12+: very strong, a monsoon surge or a nearby typhoon.
  • "from ESE (122°)" means the wind comes from that direction (0° = north, 90° = east, 180° = south, 270° = west).
  • "24h u change" = how the east–west wind changed in the last 24 h. Positive = turning more westerly; negative = more easterly. Big swings (4+ m/s) mean the pattern is changing.

Rain next 24 h and RH850

  • Model rain total for Bangkok: under 1 mm = essentially dry; 1–10 mm = light/moderate; 10–50 mm = heavy; 50+ mm = flood risk. Bangkok drains are often said to struggle above roughly 60 mm in one hour.
  • Models smooth over a ~25 km grid, so they often underestimate single thunderstorm cells. Treat as a hint, and check your rain gauge page.
  • RH850 = humidity at 1.5 km. Under 60% = dry layer, rain unlikely. 60–80% = moist. Over 85% = deep moisture, favourable for rain.

High − Low pressure (hPa)

Sea-level pressure over south-central China (30°N, 110°E) minus the Bay of Bengal (15°N, 90°E). It is a rough index of the winter monsoon, not an official one.

  • Below ~5, or negative: no real China high; typical of the SW monsoon season, when the continent is warm and pressure over it is low.
  • 5–15: moderate northeast monsoon.
  • 15–25+: strong cold surge. Expect stronger NE winds, cooler dry air in Bangkok and rough seas in the Gulf. Look for the regime turning NE a day or two later.

Nearest cyclone

  • Distance from Bangkok. Over 2,000 km: little direct effect. 1,000–2,000 km: can shift Bangkok's winds toward easterly and pull moisture. Under 1,000 km: direct influence. Storms crossing Vietnam, Laos or Cambodia can bring rain to Thailand 1–3 days later.
  • Bearing = compass direction from Bangkok (62° = east-northeast).
  • The alert colour (Green/Orange/Red) from GDACS reflects the possible humanitarian impact, not how close it is to Thailand.

MJO / ENSO / IOD (from your macro page)

  • MJO shows as phase · amplitude. Amplitude under 1 = weak/inactive; over 1 = active, the stronger the better defined. Phases 2–5 (active rain area over the Indian Ocean to Indonesia) generally favour wetter conditions near Thailand; phases 6–8 and 1 tend to favour drier. The link varies by season.
  • Niño 3.4 (ENSO): above +0.5 °C = El Niño (tends to be drier/hotter in Thailand); below −0.5 °C = La Niña (tends to be wetter); in between = neutral.
  • DMI (IOD): above +0.4 = positive phase (tends toward drier Southeast Asia); below −0.4 = negative (tends wetter). These are tendencies over months, not day-to-day forecasts.

ℹ️ How to read the regime strip

Each thin block is one hour, from 3 days ago to 7 days ahead (Bangkok time). Colours are the regimes above. The black outline is now, to the left is the past, and to the right is the model forecast. Look for long single-colour runs (a stable pattern) and colour changes (a shift in the air flow). The forecast side gets less reliable the further out it goes.

ℹ️ How to read the two charts

Wind & rain chart

  • Blue line (u850), east–west wind. Above 0 the air comes from the west (monsoon-type, from the Andaman Sea). Below 0 it comes from the east. About ±5 is moderate, ±10 strong, ±15 very strong.
  • Red line (v850), north–south wind. Above 0 it comes from the south (Gulf moisture, rain-favourable). Below 0 it comes from the north (drier, cooler).
  • Blue bars are model rain per hour for Bangkok (right-hand scale). The dashed vertical line is now.
  • Rain is most likely when the lines cross 0 or change direction, since that is where air masses meet, and when the south wind strengthens.

Pressure chart

The same High − Low pressure index as above, from 3 days ago to 7 days ahead. A rising line means the China high is building and a NE surge is on the way. A falling line means the surge is fading.

Regimes use the 850 hPa wind direction (from): SW 200–290° = westerly monsoon from Andaman/Bay of Bengal; NE 20–90° = China/Siberian-high surge; E 90–200° = Pacific/typhoon-fed easterlies; N = continental northerlies; WEAK <2.5 m/s. A bigger high–low pressure difference means a stronger NE surge. Data: Open-Meteo (GFS/ECMWF), GDACS, plus your data.json. Model output, not an official forecast.

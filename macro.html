What do these indices mean? (ranges and thresholds)

850 hPa trade wind anomaly (CPAC850) (range -10 to +10 m/s)

Lower-troposphere zonal wind anomaly across the Central Pacific (175°W–140°W) at about 1.5 km altitude. Per NOAA CPC index conventions:

  • Above +1.5 m/s (La Niña signal): strong easterly trade winds push warm ocean water westward toward Southeast Asia, amplifying monsoon rainfall in northern and central Thailand.
  • Below -1.5 m/s (El Niño signal): westerly wind anomalies weaken the trade winds, suppressing monsoon moisture transport and raising drought risk.
  • -1.5 to +1.5 m/s: neutral trade wind conditions.

Tahiti–Darwin Southern Oscillation Index (SOI) (range -3.0 to +3.0)

Standardized air pressure difference between Tahiti (East Pacific) and Darwin, Australia (West Pacific). Standardized monthly values:

  • Above +0.8 (La Niña phase): high pressure over the East Pacific and low pressure over Indonesia and Thailand drive the Walker cell's rising branch over the Gulf of Thailand.
  • Below -0.8 (El Niño phase): low pressure shifts eastward into the Central Pacific, causing subsidence and dry conditions over Indochina.
  • -0.8 to +0.8: neutral pressure gradient.

Central Pacific OLR anomaly (outgoing longwave radiation) (range -50 to +50 W/m²)

Thermal radiation emitted into space from the equatorial atmosphere (160°E–160°W). Anomaly values:

  • Above +10 W/m² (suppressed cloudiness in the Central Pacific): clear skies there correspond to enhanced convection and heavy monsoon cloud buildup over Thailand.
  • Below -10 W/m² (enhanced cloudiness in the Central Pacific): deep convective clouds shift eastward, starving the Asian monsoon of moisture.
  • -10 to +10 W/m²: normal equatorial cloud cover.

0–300 m upper-ocean heat content anomaly (range -4.0 to +4.0 °C)

Total thermal energy stored in the upper 300 metres of the equatorial Pacific (180°W–100°W):

  • Below -0.5 °C (subsurface cold pool): a shallower thermocline in the East Pacific sustains La Niña conditions and prolonged wet spells in Greater Bangkok.
  • Above +0.5 °C (subsurface warm pool): warm Kelvin waves propagate eastward, maintaining El Niño warming and lower dam inflows.
  • -0.5 to +0.5 °C: normal subsurface thermal state.

Climate indices come from NOAA CPC, the Australian Bureau of Meteorology and NOAA PSL; dams and the Pak Nam tide from Thai government hydrology services. Slow climate signals are not a forecast for tomorrow's rain.
